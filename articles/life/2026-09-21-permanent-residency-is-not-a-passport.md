# Permanent Residency Is Not a Passport

**Date:** 2026-09-21

---

This isn't really related to Europe
but I'm writing it for whoever it reaches
Permanent residency is not a passport
Any visa or permanent residency
only allows you to enter that country
It doesn't come with a ride out in times of crisis

If all you need is somewhere to land for a while
a Taiwanese passport gets you three months visa-free in lots of places, like the EU and Japan

In reality, more than sorting out your residency status
what you really need to solve is the money problem
How much deployable assets you have overseas is an extraordinarily important question

My own approach
if you set residency status aside for now
there are only two things that matter most, and they're also the simplest:

1. At home, offset your assets against your liabilities as much as possible.

2. Spread out your liquid assets and move them overseas, for example to a US broker
TSMC, most Taiwanese people's favorite, has a US-listed counterpart too: TSM
Yes, there's a price gap, but that gap is just a small cost for keeping your money safe
(Though sending money abroad only to turn around and buy Taiwanese assets is a whole other topic)

The exchange rate is a minor issue
because once you factor in geopolitics
all those FX losses are insurance premiums
And they're not necessarily losses at all
you might even come out ahead

People who go all-in on NT dollars and people who go all-in on US dollars are the same
they're just betting on different sides
Nobody is more conservative or more of a gambler
Everyone's a gambler!

Some doors, while they're wide open and you can come and go freely, nobody pays much attention to
But remember, China is a country with foreign-exchange controls
When the situation suddenly changes, onshore and offshore assets will be in worlds-apart positions
