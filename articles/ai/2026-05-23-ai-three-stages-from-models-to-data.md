# The Underlying Logic: AI's Three Stages, from Models to Data

**Date:** 2026-05-23

---

The underlying logic: the three stages of AI development
2010-2022 AI = Model Innovation, driven by architectural breakthroughs
2022-2026 AI = Compute Scaling, the compute arms race
2026-2035 AI = Data Monopoly, where data monopolies decide who wins
Why is the third stage the most critical?
*real-time physical world data* has three irreplaceable properties:
1. *Temporal Irreversibility*: you can't reshoot yesterday's satellite imagery or weather data. The past is the past
2. *Sensor Infrastructure Moat*: you can't launch 200 satellites overnight. Physical infrastructure = a time barrier
3. *Network Effect*: more data → more accurate AI → more customers → more data = data flywheel

## From Oil Economy to Data Economy

The oil era: whoever controls the oil fields → controls energy → controls the economy
The data era: whoever controls real-world data → controls AI → controls decisions

**Part 2**

AI competition is going to shift from model competition to data competition
Data is the new oil, and real world data is the scarcest kind

### The Complete AI Data Supply Chain

A lot of people only see OpenAI / Anthropic / Google, but that's just the model layer
The full industry structure looks like this:

```
┌──
│ AI Applications │ ← Application layer (ChatGPT, Copilot, all kinds of AI agents)
├──
│ AI Models │ ← Model layer (already open source; I don't invest here)
├──
│ Compute Infrastructure │ ← Compute layer (GPU cloud, ASIC)
├──
│ Data Infrastructure │ ← Data infrastructure layer (platform, fusion, orchestration)
├──
│ Real-world Data Collection │ ← Real-world data collection (hardest to replicate = most valuable)
└──
```

Investment logic: the deeper the layer, the more valuable it is, because the deeper you go, the less replaceable it gets

2. RF / Signal Intelligence: radio/signals intelligence (defense/shipping/aircraft tracking)
3. Mobility Data: transport/logistics/robot movement data
4. Climate / Weather Data: real-time weather/ocean/environmental data
5. Financial Risk Data: real-time financial risk/credit/insurance data

## Company Overview

Planet Labs operates the world's largest commercial Earth-observation satellite constellation, imaging the entire planet every single day. At its core, Planet is "a real-time sensor network for the Earth" — its data can't be recaptured or copied, which gives it a natural monopoly.

## Why It Has Dominance in AI Real-Time Data

### Core Logic

```
satellite raw data → AI interpretation → decision system
```

1. **A monopoly on Earth data**: the world's largest commercial satellite constellation, with daily global coverage. That's a moat at the physical level — you can't launch 200+ satellites overnight to compete

2. **temporal data can't be recaptured**: yesterday's satellite imagery can't be reshot. That "irreversibility" turns the historical data archive into an asset in its own right

3. **The eyes of AI**: agricultural AI, supply-chain monitoring, military intelligence, climate models — every AI that needs to "see the Earth" depends on satellite data

4. **Defense contracts accelerating**: in 2026-03 it was selected as a prime contractor on the Missile Defense Agency's SHIELD IDIQ contract, with a contract ceiling as high as $151B

## Catalysts

1. **Defense AI budgets exploding**: AI military spending by the US and its allies keeps accelerating, and Planet is a direct beneficiary
2. **agriculture AI adoption**: precision agriculture needs daily satellite imagery, and Planet is the only player that can deliver it
3. **Climate AI modeling**: quantifying ESG / climate risk takes long-term Earth-observation data, and Planet's historical archive can't be replaced
4. **data flywheel accelerating**: every extra day of imaging makes the historical archive more valuable

## Risks

1. **Not yet profitable**: satellite launches and maintenance are expensive, so losses will continue in the near term
2. **Government contract concentration**: too dependent on US government contracts
3. **Competitors catching up**: Maxar (acquired), Airbus Defence, ICEYE and others are all doubling down on satellite imagery
4. **Technology risk**: satellite hardware has a limited lifespan, so new satellites have to keep going up
