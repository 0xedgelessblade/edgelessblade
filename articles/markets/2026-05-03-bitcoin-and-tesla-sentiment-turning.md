# Bitcoin and Tesla: Sentiment May Be Quietly Turning

**Date:** 2026-05-03

---

The two weakest groups in US stocks may be quietly turning around on sentiment
Bitcoin and Tesla
For the past six months the market has treated them as invisible, but if you only watch the price and not the structure, you'll miss three things that are happening right now

Signal one: Morgan Stanley steps into bitcoin

On 4/8 Morgan Stanley launched its own MSBT, the first spot BTC ETF issued by a major US bank, and it pulled in over 100 million in its first week

Launching your own ETF in the worst market sentiment isn't retail bottom-fishing; it's yet another Wall Street giant entering the game

BlackRock's IBIT now holds more than 810,000 BTC
When the world's largest asset manager and its largest investment bank are both adding
do you want to stand with market sentiment, or with the institutions?

Signal two: the uncertainty around the CLARITY Act is narrowing

The CLARITY Act's stablecoin yield provision
the biggest dispute that held up the whole industry for six months
finally got a compromise text on 5/1: it bans "earning interest just for holding," but keeps reward mechanisms tied to real activity

Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott says the bill is already "in the red zone," aiming for markup by mid-May. Over the same period, DeFi lost $630 million to hacks in April.

But BTC still rose 12% in April, closing near $78K. When a string of industry setbacks no longer moves mainstream crypto, the worst of the sentiment may already be behind us

Signal three: Tesla's last mile

By the end of April, Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi fleet had climbed from single digits to 25 cars, deployed across three Texas cities. The number is small, but the growth is exponential

In the Q1 earnings, Musk confirmed the target of launching consumer unsupervised FSD in Q4, with plans to expand to a dozen or so states by year-end

FSD is the one variable that lifts Tesla's valuation from "carmaker" to "platform"
The stock is at about $391 now, and the CyberCab entering its production ramp early may be a sign
that large-scale rollout really isn't far off
