A few friends who really know their stuff pointed out the problem of blockchain sync latency across interplanetary distances
As it happens, I wrote up a discussion of exactly that late last year
.
Let me cut straight to the conclusion
Low Earth orbit = no problem at all
Lunar orbit = almost no problem at all
Mars orbit = the latency is a disaster
A separate Bitcoin main chain would have to be forked to run locally

Quoting my earlier post (2026-01-08): https://www.threads.com/@edgeless.blade/post/DTPFOUsAlYB

> While the market is still guessing when bitcoin will break 100k
> my mind has already drifted to a question nobody is talking about:
>
> If there were BTC mining rigs on Mars
> a block mined there could only sync with Earth 24 minutes later
> Bitcoin consensus would simply break down
>
> Here is the paper, laid out
> Since bitcoin was born in 2009
> the design of its underlying consensus mechanism has assumed an Earth-scale communication environment:
> block propagation times of about 6.5 seconds (median) to 12.6 seconds (mean)
> far below the 10-minute block interval.
>
> But once human civilization expands to Mars, this basic assumption will collapse completely
> According to NASA and ESA data, the one-way light-speed delay between Mars and Earth ranges from 3.5 minutes (at closest approach) to 24 minutes (at farthest), averaging about 13 minutes, and the round-trip communication delay can reach 48 minutes. This means a block found by a Martian miner could already be two or more blocks behind the Earth network by the time it reaches Earth.
>
> The core questions of this study are:
> How will Bitcoin's consensus mechanism fail under the extreme communication delay between Mars and Earth?
> What are the possible technical solutions?
>
> TL;DR answer:
> A dual-chain sidechain architecture
> Mars runs its own independent "MarsBTC" sidechain
> which syncs a Merkle Root with Earth's main chain periodically (every 6 hours)
> Cross-chain transfers have to wait for the sync cycle.
