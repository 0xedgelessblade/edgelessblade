# Good Product ≠ Good Business ≠ Good Stock

**Date:** 2026-09-13

---

You like a company's products
so you buy the company's stock
Is that wrong?

The principle isn't wrong

But it's only the starting point
Next comes due diligence

Gate one:
Compared with other products in the same space,
is it really still outstanding?

If so
it has at least cleared the "good product" gate

Then on to gate two:
Is this an emerging space that's still growing?
Or one that's already mature, even declining,
and packed with competitors?

Here's a very simple example.

A foreign investor comes to Taiwan
and drinks a random cup of bubble tea

Holy cow, mind blown
He immediately buys a franchise and opens a shop of his own

And it goes under
Why?

Because he opened a no-name brand
with no edge whatsoever over the competition

Where did he die this round?
Not enough judgment of his own

What he thought was a "good product"
once put up against the whole market
wasn't a top product at all

Later the foreigner finally understands the industry
This time he opens a 50 Lan (五十嵐, the Taiwanese bubble tea chain) in Taipei

That's a good enough product, right?
It still went under in the end

Why?

Because a good product
doesn't mean the business will be easy

When a space is already highly mature
and strong competitors are everywhere,
even the best product can get eaten alive by price wars and me-too competition

In the end the foreigner, heartbroken,
goes back home abroad
and opens yet another bubble tea shop

And it's a smash hit!!

Why?
Same good product,
but this time it entered a space that's still growing locally,
with far less competition than in Taiwan

**Part 2**

It really is that simple

"I like this product"
can be the starting point for discovering a company

But after that you still need to ask, at the very least:
Compared with the competition, is it really strong enough?

Does this space still have room to grow?

Can the company's business model turn its product edge into profit?

And finally
for a company this good
has the share price already priced in all of that good news?

Good product ≠ good business
Good business ≠ good stock

A good product,
in a good space,
run by a good company,
and finally at a reasonable price

That's what makes a complete investment
